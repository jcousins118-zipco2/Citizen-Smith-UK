Damp and Mould Complaint

Use when: damp, mould, or condensation is affecting the property and the landlord is not dealing with it properly.

The likely position

Under the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 (s.11), your landlord has a duty to keep the structure and exterior of the property in repair — this includes damp-proof courses, roofs, gutters, and plumbing that could cause damp. Under the Homes (Fitness for Human Habitation) Act 2018, the property must be fit to live in throughout the tenancy. Damp and mould is a prescribed Category 1 hazard under the Housing Health and Safety Rating System (HHSRS), created by the Housing Act 2004. If the problem is serious, you can ask the council environmental health team to inspect and potentially issue an improvement notice.

What to keep

  • photos of the mould or damp
  • dates it was reported
  • medical or health impact notes
  • any messages blaming you without proper inspection

Template wording

If they keep going

Formal escalation to the landlord's complaints process, then council environmental health (for HHSRS enforcement) or the Housing Ombudsman (for social landlords). In serious cases the council can issue an improvement notice and carry out work.
