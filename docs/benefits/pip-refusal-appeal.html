PIP Refusal / Appeal

Use when: PIP has been refused, reduced, or stopped and you believe the decision was wrong.

The likely position

Under the Welfare Reform Act 2012 (s.102) and the Social Security (Decisions and Appeals) Regulations 1999 (reg.3), you may be able to request a mandatory reconsideration (a revision of the decision). You normally need to do this within 1 month of the decision date. If the mandatory reconsideration is refused, you can appeal to the independent First-tier Tribunal (Social Security Chamber). Tribunal success rates are high — about 70% of PIP appeals are successful in whole or in part.

What to keep

  • the PIP decision letter
  • daily living or mobility diaries
  • medical or professional evidence
  • any assessment report if you have it

If they keep going

If the mandatory reconsideration is refused, you have 1 month to appeal to the First-tier Tribunal. Tribunal appeals are handled by HMCTS — they are free and independent of the DWP. Most people are better off with specialist help at this stage (Citizens Advice, a law centre, or a welfare rights adviser).
