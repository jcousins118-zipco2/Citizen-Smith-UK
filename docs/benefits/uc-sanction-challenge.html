UC Sanction Challenge

Use when: your Universal Credit has been sanctioned and you believe the decision was wrong or that you had a good reason (good cause).

The likely position

Under the Universal Credit Regulations 2013 (reg.99-113), the DWP can reduce your UC if you fail to meet a work-related requirement without good reason. However, you may be able to challenge the sanction if you had good cause (e.g. illness, caring responsibilities, mental health crisis, transport problems, or communication issues). The DWP must consider your individual circumstances, including any health or vulnerability. If the sanction is causing hardship, you can ask for a hardship payment.

What to keep

  • the sanction decision letter
  • reasons or evidence for what happened
  • health or vulnerability context
  • the claimant commitment if available

If they keep going

If the DWP does not change the decision, you can appeal to the First-tier Tribunal. Hardship payments may be available while you wait — ask through your UC journal or by calling the DWP. A charity or Citizens Advice can help with the appeal.
