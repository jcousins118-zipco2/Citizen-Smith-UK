Redundancy

Use when: you are being made redundant and believe the process was unfair — e.g. the selection criteria were wrong, you were not consulted, suitable alternatives were not offered, or the redundancy is not genuine.

The likely position

Under the Employment Rights Act 1996 (s.139), redundancy is defined as dismissal wholly or mainly attributable to the employer ceasing or reducing the need for work of a particular kind. The redundancy must be genuine. If it is not genuine — or if the selection process, consultation, or alternative job offer process was unfair — you may be able to challenge it under the unfair dismissal provisions (ERA 1996 s.98). You normally need 2 years of continuous service for a redundancy unfair dismissal claim. If 20 or more staff are affected, collective consultation rules apply.

What to keep

  • the redundancy letter
  • selection criteria used
  • any alternatives offered
  • bumping documentation if applicable

Template wording

If they keep going

If the employer does not resolve the grievance, go to ACAS early conciliation then employment tribunal. Redundancy pay and notice entitlements also need checking. If 20+ staff are being made redundant in a 90-day period, collective consultation rules apply — a trade union may be involved.
