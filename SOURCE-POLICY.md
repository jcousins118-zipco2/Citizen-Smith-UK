# Citizen Smith — Research & Source Policy

## Purpose
Citizen Smith must not invent legal positions, copy whole third-party sites, or blur law, guidance, and opinion. This policy defines what sources we use, how we use them, and how content is approved for publication.

---

## 1. Source Hierarchy

### Tier 1 — Primary / strongest authority
Use first wherever possible.

- legislation.gov.uk
- GOV.UK
- FCA Handbook / FCA official pages
- Ofgem official pages
- Ofcom official pages
- Ombudsman official pages
- ACAS official guidance
- HMCTS / tribunal official procedure pages

**Use for:**
- legal rules
- statutory language
- regulator obligations
- official escalation process
- deadlines and formal procedure

### Tier 2 — Trusted support guidance
Use for practical interpretation and citizen-friendly explanation.

- Citizens Advice
- StepChange
- National Debtline
- Shelter
- MoneyHelper / Money and Pensions Service
- CAP UK
- other approved UK non-profit or official support bodies

**Use for:**
- plain-English interpretation
- citizen action steps
- practical process advice
- template inspiration

### Tier 3 — Citizen Smith synthesis
Our role is to combine Tier 1 and Tier 2 into:
- plain-English summaries
- issue maps
- template letters
- escalation guides
- evidence checklists

Citizen Smith content must never outrank or contradict Tier 1.

---

## 2. What We May Publish

Each issue page should contain:

1. **Our summary**
   - rewritten in plain English
   - short, accurate, neutral

2. **Authority block**
   - short attributed quote where useful
   - regulation number / statute / official guidance name
   - direct source link

3. **Action block**
   - what the user can do next
   - who to contact
   - what document/template to use

4. **Escalation block**
   - ombudsman / tribunal / regulator / complaint path

5. **Last checked date**
   - visible on every page

---

## 3. What We Must NOT Do

- Do not copy full articles or large sections from third-party sites
- Do not republish whole guidance pages as our own
- Do not present charity guidance as if it is binding law
- Do not present Citizen Smith text as legal advice
- Do not publish unsourced legal claims
- Do not use stale undated process information
- Do not merge conflicting sources without flagging the conflict

---

## 4. Quoting Rules

Allowed:
- short quotations from official or approved sites
- brief extracts needed to support a legal point
- attributed excerpts with source URL and organisation name

Preferred method:
- quote the minimum necessary
- rewrite the rest in our own words
- link to the original page

Every quote should be logged with:
- page title
- organisation
- URL
- date checked
- reason used

---

## 5. Research Workflow

### Step 1 — Find source
Research agent identifies candidate sources.

### Step 2 — Classify source
Tag each source as:
- law
- regulator
- ombudsman procedure
- support guidance
- commentary

### Step 3 — Extract only what is needed
Pull:
- exact regulation references
- short quotable text
- process steps
- deadlines
- escalation paths

### Step 4 — Create source record
For each issue page, maintain:
- issue name
- sector
- primary source(s)
- secondary source(s)
- approved quote(s)
- date checked
- confidence notes
- linked templates

### Step 5 — Rewrite for publication
Citizen Smith publishes a short, readable synthesis — not a source dump.

### Step 6 — Review
Before a page goes live:
- source links checked
- quotes checked
- legal position checked against primary source
- template checked against source record

---

## 6. Page Content Standard

Every issue page should use the same structure:

- What is happening?
- What rule may apply?
- Why it matters
- What to say
- What evidence to gather
- Who to escalate to
- Sources
- Last checked

This keeps the site scalable across debt, housing, energy, telecoms, benefits, and employment.

---

## 7. Template Approval Standard

A template can only be published if it has:
- a linked issue page
- at least one Tier 1 or strong process source
- clear placeholders instead of personal data
- a stated use case
- a stated escalation path

Templates should be classed as:
- quick send
- formal complaint
- regulatory escalation
- evidence log
- timeline / case summary

---

## 8. Consent / Disclaimer Position

Citizen Smith should require a clear user acknowledgement that:
- it is an information and document-support platform
- it is not a law firm
- it does not store personal case data unless explicitly stated later
- users should remove names, addresses, account numbers, DOBs, and other identifiers before pasting text
- users remain responsible for what they send

This disclaimer supports the platform, but does not replace careful sourcing.

---

## 9. Expansion Rule

We do not expand into a new sector until we have:
- source register created
- top 5 common issue types defined
- top official sources identified
- top support sites identified
- at least 3 templates mapped

Suggested expansion order:
1. Debt & credit
2. Housing
3. Energy
4. Telecoms
5. Benefits
6. Employment

---

## 10. Operating Principle

Citizen Smith does not win by claiming to know everything.
Citizen Smith wins by:
- being accurate
- being sourced
- being readable
- being structured
- showing citizens exactly which rule, source, and next step matters
