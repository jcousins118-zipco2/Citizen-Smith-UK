About Citizen Smith

Citizen Smith is a free rights-and-action platform for people under pressure.

It is designed for people dealing with institutions while stressed, overwhelmed, vulnerable, low on confidence, or simply exhausted.

The aim is simple: help people understand what may apply, use the right wording, keep the right evidence, and escalate properly if needed.

How it works

  1. You pick what type of problem you have.
  2. You describe what the other side is saying or doing.
  3. We show the rule or position that likely applies.
  4. We give wording you can use in an email, letter, webform, live chat, or phone call.
  5. We show what evidence to keep and where to go next if things don't get resolved.

Where you can, keep a written record of everything.

Where the information comes from

Every route on this site is sourced from a regulation, statutory instrument, or official guidance. We link to the source so you can check it yourself. Key sources include:

  • UK legislation (legislation.gov.uk)
  • FCA Handbook (CONC rules)
  • Ofgem licence conditions and guidance
  • Ofcom regulations and dispute codes
  • Equality Act 2010
  • Employment Rights Act 1996
  • Landlord and Tenant Act 1985
  • DWP decision-making guidance
  • Ombudsman schemes and ADR bodies

What Citizen Smith is for

  • Making confusing situations easier to understand
  • Turning a problem into a practical next step
  • Helping you write, send, or say the right thing
  • Showing what evidence matters if the problem continues

What Citizen Smith is not

  • Not a law firm and does not provide legal advice
  • Not a legal referral funnel or paid service
  • Not a chatbot pretending to know everything
  • Not a guarantee — some situations need a solicitor, especially if urgent, severe, or already in court

Privacy

Citizen Smith does not collect, store, or share any of your personal information. There is no sign-up, no tracking, and no data entry on this site — you read the guidance, then take action on your own.

Core values

  • The user comes first
  • Rights guidance should stay free
  • Plain English matters
  • Vulnerable people deserve systems that make sense
  • Trust matters more than short-term money
